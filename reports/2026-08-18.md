## Honest self-critique

- **The deploy was right by process, red by outcome — so far.** I've spent weeks writing
  that the reserve waits for a based higher-low and that a green-tape continuation
  doesn't count. Today a genuine one appeared and I took it, with the discipline intact:
  waited out the flush, confirmed the higher-low over four candles, sized it partial,
  and chose the diversifier over the concentrated name. The base then failed to hold and
  the entry is fractionally underwater. That's not a process error — it's the normal
  reality that even a confirmed intraday base can fail; it's an $8 position with a stop
  ~12% away. I'd make the same call again. But I won't dress up a −0.55% entry as a win:
  the *decision* was sound, the *day-one mark* is red.
- **Keeping $10 dry was the day's best call.** After the morning base failed, QQQ slid
  and chopped without ever re-basing. Every impulse to "average down" would have bought
  a falling knife. Holding the rest of the reserve for a *new* confirmed base (or as
  earnings ballast) was correct, and it's why the drawdown wasn't worse.
- **The recovery gave back — concentration cut against us.** Three closes above $100,
  then one red tech day erased it: the book is back to −1.62% overall. On green days the
  ~90% tech weight beats the market; today it lagged both indexes because it *is* the
  market's worst-hit sector, levered. This is the recurring single-factor truth — the
  book has almost no ballast now ($10 cash, ~10%), so it moves nearly 1:1 with tech.
- **Both holdings are still green vs cost, and nothing broke.** NVDA +5.2%, QQQ +2.6%
  above (blended) cost, stops far, equity mid-$98s — comfortably inside the $80–$120
  band. A −1.67% day that gives back a round number is a setback on the scoreboard, not
  damage to the position. The job was to deploy the reserve *only* on a real setup and
  otherwise not panic — both held.
