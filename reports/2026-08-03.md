- **The recovery is real, and NVDA is leading it.** This is the constructive
  development the last two weeks lacked: NVDA reclaimed *and held* $200,
  closing +3.12% and back essentially at our $208.68 cost. QQQ is back above

- **The 19% cash lagged again — same tension as July 30.** On a one-way-up day,
  holding $18 dry meant the book (+1.90%) trailed the pure Nasdaq (+2.13%). The
  deploy rule correctly refuses to *chase*, and no valid higher-low ever formed

  nothing on a day price simply never pulls back. The discipline that saved us
  on the downside (Wed's FOMC knee-jerk) costs relative gains on a clean up day.
  Both are real; it's the trade-off of demanding proof before deploying.
- **My pre-market read was too bearish.** I called NVDA's −1% pre-market a
  possible bleed toward the mid-$190s; it was a head-fake and NVDA led +3%.
  The lesson isn't "be more bullish" — it's that a 1% pre-market wiggle carries
  almost no signal, and I over-weighted it. The *plan* (hold) was insulated
  from the bad read, which is exactly why the plan is HOLD and not "predict the
  open."
- **Still a two-name, high-beta book.** NVDA (~31%) and QQQ (~35%) are both
  directional tech; the $18 cash (19%) is the only non-correlated ballast and
  the only lever left. The recovery has been kind to that concentration — but
  the same concentration is what dug the −10% hole. The reserve's job remains:
  convert to a position on a *real* setup, or keep protecting optionality.
